# Scout brief — issue-1

Mode: parallel WebSearch fan-out, 4 angles in one turn (stage 1), 1 deepening
round (stage 2), stopped at saturation. 2 stages used of 5-stage budget.

## Angles run

1. Textbook/industry framework (Porter five forces, JTBD, general templates)
2. Top consulting-firm deliverable structure (McKinsey/BCG/Bain)
3. Market-sizing methodology (TAM/SAM/SOM, evidence standards)
4. Practitioner (PM/startup) competitive-analysis template norms

Deepening round: evidence/citation rigor standards for market research reports.

## Must-bes (Kano) — what strong market-analysis work assumes

- **Multi-framework layering, not one framework.** No single tool (five forces,
  JTBD, SWOT) captures the full picture; strong practice deliberately combines
  frameworks that each answer a different question (industry attractiveness vs.
  positioning vs. customer need) rather than picking one and stopping.
- **Bottom-up sizing preferred over top-down-only.** Top-down (analyst-report)
  TAM is fast but weakly defensible; bottom-up (unit economics, traceable
  assumptions) is the credibility bar. Best practice runs both and reconciles.
- **Every claim traces to a source, and sources are graded.** Rigor = named
  data source + method transparency (sample, collection method) + cross-
  referencing multiple independent sources before trusting a number.
- **Deliverable is recommendation-first, not narrative-first.** Top consulting
  output leads with the decision-relevant finding and action, using MECE
  structure — not a chronological account of what was analyzed.
- **Recurring, not one-shot.** Competitive analysis is treated as a practice
  that gets re-run as the market moves, not a single frozen artifact.

## Performance axes (where strong work visibly competes)

1. **Evidence traceability** — bottom-up, sourced, cross-referenced vs. asserted numbers.
2. **Framework fit-for-question** — matching the framework to the decision it must inform (attractiveness vs. positioning vs. need), not framework-for-framework's-sake.
3. **Action-orientation** — findings phrased as a decision input, not a survey report.

## Adopt / skip

- **Adopt:** required-evidence-per-claim (source name + method note), bottom-up-first sizing where sizing is in scope, MECE / recommendation-first structure for the proposal and the record.
- **Skip:** full SWOT-per-competitor and heavyweight persona/customer-interview primary research — out of proportion for a phase-1 proposal-only pass; this role's `PRODUCES` scope (five-forces, competitor list, JTBD-landscape verdict) does not currently include a market-sizing or persona component, so TAM/SAM/SOM and deep customer-interview methodology are not being pulled in as new required components — flagged as an option for the approver, not silently adopted.

## Segment fit

This role hands off to `pricing` and `marketing` once a spec-vs-competition
verdict is reached — its scope is closer to the "is this defensible against
competitors" consulting-analyst segment than to the market-sizing/VC-pitch
segment. The must-bes above are weighted accordingly (evidence rigor and
framework fit over TAM/SAM/SOM).

## Gap line

Current state (directive.sh) already asserts the *names* of three outputs
(five-forces, competitor list, JTBD verdict) that match the field's own
must-bes for framework choice — this part needs a rationale write-up, not a
framework change. What is missing entirely: evidence-traceability requirement
(no current field mandates sourcing or grading), and any structural norm for
the phase-1 proposal document itself (no MECE/recommendation-first requirement
exists anywhere in this repo).
